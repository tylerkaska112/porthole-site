For iPhone and iPad

Windows PC games, in your pocket.

Porthole runs Windows games on your iPhone and iPad. Bring your own game files, download the ones you own on Steam, Epic Games, GOG and itch.io, and see how each game runs on phones like yours before you try it.

Get Porthole See what runs
Early hobby project. Porthole isn't on the App Store and there's nothing to download yet. It changes often, and plenty of games won't run.

Try it

Tap around a Porthole

A working copy of the iPhone app's layout, with made-up games and no connection to anything. Open a store, start a download, press Play, then try Settings › Appearance: it changes the colours of this whole website.

Things to try

  • Open Stores, sign in to GOG and install a game. The round button shows progress.
  • Start two downloads, then pause one or move another to the front on the Downloads page.
  • Press Play, switch the power mode, and trigger the low-memory warning.
  • Open a game's page and try How it runs.
  • In Settings › Appearance, pick a theme, or your own colour.

Everything in the demo is invented, and it shows the layout, not how any game runs. See pictures of the real app.

Before you start

Will it work for me?

Porthole asks a bit more of you than an App Store app. If you can tick these four, you can use it. The details are on the Get it page, and the setup checker tells you what is missing.

An iPhone or iPad on iOS 18 or later

More memory is better: a game that needs more than your device has can't run.

A way to sign the app

Porthole isn't on the App Store, so you sign it yourself. A paid developer account or a third-party certificate service is recommended and gives you everything. A free Apple ID works with some features missing.

A way to switch on JIT

Porthole does it itself once it has a pairing file. On iOS 27 or later your device can make that file on its own; before that it is made once on a computer.

Games you own

From Steam, Epic Games, GOG or itch.io, or game folders you copy in from Files. Porthole contains no games.

What it does

Everything in one place

Add games your way

Copy a game folder in from the Files app, or run a Windows installer. Porthole finds the right program in the folder and keeps uninstallers and helper tools out of the way.

Four stores, one place

Sign in and download the Windows games you own from Steam, Epic Games, GOG or itch.io. Each store has a grid of your games and a page for every game, every game shows which store it came from, and one Downloads page shows what all of them are downloading.

Downloads that keep going

Leave the app and the queue carries on. A card on the lock screen shows the percentage, size and speed, with buttons to pause, resume or cancel.

Built for a controller

Move around the whole app with a game controller, like a console. PlayStation and Xbox controllers work without setup, the on-screen buttons hide while one is in use, and every game's buttons can be remapped. Steam says which games support one, and Porthole shows it on every game.

Know before you try

Each game shows a badge from reports by other people: works great, works with problems, or doesn't work. Add your own in under a minute.

Made for iPhone and iPad

Touch controls you can switch while you play, a page for every game with its own settings, and a layout that fills the iPad screen.

No ads, no account

Porthole has no ads and no account of its own. A compatibility report is only sent when you choose to send one, and nothing in it names you.

Make it yours

Build your own controls

Porthole's on-screen controls are yours to arrange. This is a small copy of the editor: use the stick and buttons, then press Edit to drag things around (they snap to guides), add more, and undo.

Only a demo: nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere. In the app the layout is kept per game, and you can share one with other players.

Made to be used with a controller

A shelf that feels like a console

Your games sit on one row. Move along it and the one you're on grows, with its name, its badges and Play right there.

How it works

Three quick steps to your first game

  1. Get ready

    Install Porthole and follow the setup guide: switch on full speed, allow its private link, and download Microsoft's Windows components.

    About 2 minutes, and 25 MB

  2. Add a game

    Sign in to Steam, Epic Games, GOG or itch.io and download one you own, or copy a game folder in from Files.

    As long as the download takes

  3. Press Play

    Check the game's badge first if you like, then press Play. Tell other people how it went with a short report.

    The first start takes a minute or more

Under the hood Porthole is built on Madeira, a project that runs Windows programs on iOS by combining Wine, the FEX-Emu translator and DXMT, which turns Direct3D into Metal. Speed depends on the game and on the device, and some games need more memory than a phone has. It needs iOS 18 or later and JIT switched on, which Porthole can do by itself once it has a pairing file.

Straight talk

What to expect

It's early

Porthole is a hobby project that changes often. Plenty of games don't run yet, and some run slowly. The compatibility list shows what people have actually tried.

It takes some setting up

Porthole isn't on the App Store, because it uses interfaces Apple keeps private. You sign the app file with your own developer certificate and install it yourself. Here's what you need.

You bring the games

Porthole never includes games and doesn't get around any copy protection. You need to own what you run.

Questions

Good to know

Is Porthole on the App Store?

No, and it can't be: it relies on things Apple doesn't allow in App Store apps. You install it yourself, signed with your own certificate. The Get it page explains how.

Is it legal?

Porthole contains no games and doesn't bypass any copy protection. Steam games start through Valve's own client files, which check your licence as they do on a PC, Epic, GOG and itch.io games are installed from the stores' own servers with your own account, and Microsoft's runtime is downloaded from Microsoft. Porthole is free software under the GPL and its source code is public.

Which games will run?

It depends on the game and the device. The compatibility list comes from reports sent by people using Porthole, and the app shows the same badge on each game.

Do I need a controller?

No. Porthole has on-screen touch controls, and a keyboard works too. A controller makes it feel like a console, and Porthole shows which games support one.

Was this built with AI?

Yes. A large part of the code, the documentation, this website and the artwork was written by an AI coding assistant (Claude, from Anthropic) working with the maintainer, who directs the project, reviews the changes and tests them on real devices. The project therefore contains AI-written code, and like any software it may contain mistakes.

Does it collect my data?

No account, no ads and no tracking. A compatibility report is only sent when you choose to send one. The privacy page lists exactly what a report contains.

Is it affiliated with Valve, Microsoft or Apple?

No. Steam and its artwork belong to Valve; Epic Games, GOG and every game or launcher you run belong to their owners. Apple, iPhone, iPad and iOS are trademarks of Apple Inc.
