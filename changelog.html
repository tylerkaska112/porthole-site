What's new

What's new in Porthole

Porthole is in early development; 1.0.0 was its first public release. This is what has changed, in plain words, newest first.

1.2.0 “Autumn Tide”

A keyboard and a mouse in games, the whole app on a controller, the EA app as a sixth store, much faster Steam downloads, a new look and a newer game engine. Because the engine is new, every game shows “Not yet tested” until people have played it on 1.2.0.

Keyboard and mouse

  • A Bluetooth or USB keyboard works in games like on a PC: every key with its own press and release, Shift, Ctrl, Alt and Command (the Windows key), the keypad and function keys. Typing into a program's own text boxes works, and Command-V pastes the clipboard's text.
  • A mouse or trackpad on iPad. The game's own cursor follows the pointer in menus; left, right, middle, back and forward buttons and the wheel work. A game that hides its cursor to look around gets the mouse's movement and the pointer is locked to the game, so the camera keeps turning at the screen's edge.
  • Your own keys for Porthole: F10 opens Porthole's menu in a game and Ctrl+Alt+P locks or frees the pointer; both can be changed in Settings. Mouse speed (also per game), scroll direction, key repeat and a layout option for typing are there too.
  • The mouse wheel and press-and-drag scroll every screen, including Home's shelf of games. Right-click a game on Home for Play and Details; with a mouse, a double-click plays it.
  • An iPhone gets a mouse only through AssistiveTouch and has no pointer lock.

Controllers

  • The whole app works with a controller, including an on-screen keyboard you drive with the D-pad (A types, B deletes, X is space, Y is shift), and a strip of hints that always says what each button does.
  • Every section opens with the glow in the same place, and a slider is entered with A so passing over a setting can't change it.
  • The controller's battery reaches games that show it.

Stores

  • The EA app is the sixth store. Sign in on EA's own page, see your PC games, install them straight from EA's servers (pause, resume, update, repair, uninstall) and play them without the EA app. Games with EA's kernel anti-cheat can't run.
  • Steam is steadier and faster. It no longer signs you out at random, downloads ran at 160-170 MB/s in a test (12-22 before), and the sizes shown add up: a game's page shows the download and the size on the device.
  • Demos, betas and playtests you own show in your Steam library with a label.
  • itch.io games that come as .7z or .rar archives install, and archives inside a download are unpacked where they lie.

Games and the engine

  • A newer engine. Madeira's latest work is merged in (hundreds of upstream changes), so more games can run. Final Fantasy Resonance (and its demo), Celeste and other .NET Framework games now start.
  • Windows components: Wine Mono for .NET games, Wine Gecko for launchers that show a web page, and the 32-bit Visual C++ runtime, each downloaded from its maker, checked before use, and removable in Settings.
  • GOG Galaxy services for GOG games built with GOG's SDK, using your GOG sign-in.
  • Donor memory, optional, for big games that run out of the app's memory. It needs one more App ID when signing.
  • Sound: Game volume (50% to 200%), and a setting that brings a game's sound back after a call or Siri.
  • Per game: the graphics card it sees, gyro aim and turbo buttons, Madeira's tested setup with an Undo, and sharing a setup as a file or one line of text.
  • Experimental, off by default: keep a game running in the background for a while, and keep compiled graphics pipelines between runs.

Compatibility reports

  • The Reports tab is built around your own games, with how each runs on this version and filters for each result.
  • A report is sent with the game's log, cleaned of personal details and kept private. Every report is checked before it appears or counts toward a badge. What is sent.

Look

  • A redesigned Home, Stores page, game page, launch screen, setup guide and performance overlay, and Settings reorganised with a search box that finds any setting.

Fixed

  • Steam signing you out at random, and download sizes that didn't add up.
  • Tapping the launch screen while a game started could break the start; a failed launch's message now stays until you close it.
  • “Cellular data is off” staying on Home after cellular data was back on.
  • Many smaller fixes for specific games and for Home, Stores and Settings.

Known limits

  • Red Dead Redemption 2 is not playable yet: the Rockstar launcher signs in and starts the game, but with Rockstar's newest Social Club the game stops with a Social Club error before the main menu.
  • Call of Duty: Black Ops III did not start in testing.
  • Not yet run on a device: Donor memory, gyro aim and turbo, Madeira's tested setups, sharing a setup, GOG Galaxy services, and .7z and .rar installs. Please say if one doesn't work.

Earlier releases: 1.0 and 1.1

Stores and downloads

  • One Stores tab. Steam, Epic Games, GOG, itch.io and an All games page share it, with a switcher at the top. Every game carries the logo of the store it came from, on its card and on the home screen.
  • Epic Games. Sign in on Epic's own page, then install the games your account owns that have a Windows version and play them without Epic's launcher. Epic's overlay and anti-cheat are not supported.
  • GOG. Sign in on GOG's own page and install the games you own, straight from GOG's servers where it has a build there, otherwise from GOG's setup file. Games that need GOG Galaxy's online features are not supported.
  • itch.io. Paste a personal API key made on itch.io and install the games you bought, claimed or collected, including free ones.
  • The same layout for every store: a grid of your games with a badge for what each is doing and a page for every game, with its compatibility badge, description and screenshots where the store has them.
  • One Downloads page for every store, opened by a round button that stays at the bottom right of the Stores tab. It shows what each store is downloading, the games waiting and the paused ones, with Pause, Resume, Download now, Pause all and Resume all.
  • Downloads keep going when you leave the app, and a card on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island shows the percentage, size, speed and time left with Pause, Resume and Cancel buttons. The card is an optional part of the install; without it iOS's own progress card is used.
  • Steam games on an external drive. Choose a folder on an SSD and send new Steam downloads there. This is new and has had little testing.
  • Much less lag in the Stores tab and while something downloads, and swiping in from the left edge goes back, as in any iOS app.

Security and privacy

  • Store sign-ins stay in the iOS Keychain, for this device only. Store traffic is kept out of the network cache, and sign-in pages keep no cookies.
  • Pairing files, certificates and profiles you pick are deleted from temporary storage as soon as they are read.
  • Updates are checked against the maintainer's signature before they are installed.
  • Problem reports also hide IPv6 addresses and any password, token or key that appears next to its name, and the pairing log no longer names your iPhone.

Updating the app

  • A popup while it updates itself with the steps (download, sign, install), one progress bar, and a plain warning to keep the app open until it is done.
  • Signing is several times faster, and so is sending the signed app to iOS's installer.

The engine

  • Ten open fixes from the Madeira project were taken in, among them the reduced-precision x87 switch working as its name says, a large-memory option for games that reserve a lot at start, more of Windows' own DirectX helper libraries, and fixes for launchers that start a second copy of themselves.

Look and feel

  • A new home screen. Your games sit on one shelf. With a controller connected the chosen game grows and the rest step back; on touch every game is the same size.
  • A slim top bar on iPhone and iPad, with the Porthole mark and the name next to it.
  • A game's page is short again: Play, three settings, and four doors (controls, how it runs, more options, about and files).
  • An app icon: a silver porthole looking at a sunset sea, and a night sky with a crescent moon in dark mode.
  • Dark only, in a slate blue in the Steam spirit with Porthole's own cyan.

Controllers

  • FULL and PARTIAL badges from Steam's own list, on the shelf, the Steam screen and every game's page.
  • Press the badge to see what it means and how to cover the parts a controller can't reach.
  • A Controller tab on the Steam screen that lists only the games with controller support.

Steam

  • Add an installed game back to your library at any time; it still starts through Steam.
  • A clearer Steam screen and Downloads page, with labelled buttons and one summary line.

Compatibility reports

  • Shorter lists, with the details one switch away.
  • Reports are counted per device model and per engine version, so an old result isn't passed off as a current one.

Setup and updates

  • Setup is three steps: full speed, Windows components, your games from any of the four stores, then a short Good to know page.
  • A warning in the game when memory or the swap file is nearly full, saying what to change.
  • Settings has six pages instead of fifteen.
  • Updates: versions are listed five at a time; with no certificate added you can download any version as a file.
  • Your VPN: Porthole asks before pausing it, and can turn it back on with a shortcut you make.
  • Notices on the home screen can be hidden with a small x.

Every build, newest first

The last 25 builds, exactly as the app lists them in Settings › Updates. This list is made from the project's change log.

1.2.0 (build 681): Autumn Tide: keyboard and mouse in games, the whole app on a controller, the EA store, a new look and a new game engine.

Added

  • A keyboard and a mouse or trackpad (iPad) work in games: every key with the modifiers, five mouse buttons and the wheel, the game's own cursor in menus, a locked pointer for looking around, and your own key for Porthole's menu (F10 by default). The mouse wheel and press-and-drag scroll every screen.
  • The whole app works with a controller: an on-screen keyboard you drive with the D-pad, a fixed starting place on every screen, and live button hints.
  • The EA app joins Steam, Epic Games, GOG, itch.io and Amazon Games as a store.
  • A new look for Home, Stores, game pages, the launch screen, setup, Reports, Settings (with search) and the performance overlay.
  • Optional Donor memory for big games, gyro aim and turbo, sharing a game's setup, and .7z installs for itch.io.

Changed

  • A new game engine: Madeira's latest work is merged in, so more games can run. Compatibility badges start again at "Not yet tested" for it.
  • Steam is steadier and faster: no random sign-outs, downloads of 160-170 MB/s in testing, and sizes that add up.
  • Game reports are sent with the game's log and checked before they appear.

Fixed

  • Final Fantasy Resonance, Celeste and other .NET Framework games now run.
1.2.129 (build 680): The mouse wheel scrolls every screen again.

Added

  • Every scroll view in the app takes a mouse: press and drag scrolls it, the wheel and trackpad scroll it as iPadOS does, and shelves that only run sideways follow the wheel's up-and-down turn.

Fixed

  • 1.2.128 made the mouse wheel stop scrolling most screens (Settings, game pages, stores, sheets): the wheel handler added for Home's shelf was attached to every scroll container and competed with its own scrolling. It now acts only on views that scroll sideways and nothing else.
1.2.128 (build 679): Scroll the game shelf with a mouse, choose your own menu key, and more keyboard and mouse polish.

Added

  • Home's shelf of games scrolls with a mouse: the wheel scrolls it sideways and you can press and drag it. A big library was out of reach before. Click-and-drag also scrolls the other lists and the tab row.
  • Settings > Engine options > Keyboard and mouse > Keys for Porthole: choose the key (or key combination) that opens Porthole's menu in a game (default F10) and the one that locks or frees the pointer (default Ctrl+Alt+P). Tap a row, press the key. Ctrl+Alt+M and Ctrl+Alt+P always work too. The first few games with a keyboard say which key opens the menu.
  • Scroll direction: follow iPadOS or reversed, for games and the shelf on Home.
  • Hold a key to repeat it: Backspace, Delete, the arrows, Home, End and Page Up/Down repeat after half a second in games (a choice: all keys, or off).
  • Keyboard layout for typing: follow the keyboard's letters instead of US key positions, for typing on a French, German or other keyboard (opt-in).
  • Each game has its own Mouse speed and 'Always use the mouse to look' on its page, under Engine.
  • A notice when iPadOS will not lock the pointer (Porthole must fill the screen, not Split View or Stage Manager).
  • Right-click a game on Home has Details as well as Play; with a mouse, a double-click plays the game and a single click opens its page.
1.2.127 (build 678): Backspace in a search box no longer goes back.

Fixed

  • With a keyboard, Backspace, Space, Return and the arrows in a text box (searching for a game) moved around or went back a screen instead of editing the text. While a text box has the keyboard they are the text's.
1.2.126 (build 677): A mouse only opens things when you click.

Changed

  • With a mouse or trackpad, resting the pointer over a Settings page or a game tile only highlights it; the page opens and the game shows in the hero when you click (a controller still opens them as it moves).
1.2.125 (build 676): Escape no longer closes the game's screen.

Fixed

  • Pressing Escape on a keyboard in a game showed the game library for a moment and then went back to the game: UIKit also acted on the key and dismissed the full-screen cover the game runs in, and the app put it back. While a game has the keyboard, its UIKit key presses are now swallowed and the cover cannot be dismissed by a key.
1.2.124 (build 675): Keyboard and mouse: the game was never given focus.

Fixed

  • Keyboard and mouse in games did nothing: the game screen is shown as a full-screen cover, and the focus check took any presented screen for a sheet over the game, so keys and clicks never reached it. Only a screen over the game's own counts now. The log says why the game does not have focus (`[hwinput] keyboard focus: elsewhere (...)`).
1.2.123 (build 674): A keyboard and a mouse or trackpad work in games.

Added

  • A Bluetooth or USB keyboard works in a game like on a PC: every key with its own press and release, Shift, Ctrl, Alt and Command (the Windows key) as keys, the keypad, function keys and arrows. Typing into a program's own text fields works, and Command-V pastes the iPad's clipboard text.
  • A mouse or trackpad on iPad: the game's own cursor follows the pointer in menus, left, right, middle, back and forward buttons and the wheel work, and a game that hides its cursor to look around gets the mouse's movement with the pointer locked to the game, so the camera keeps turning at the screen edges. (Ported from Madeira's own hardware input layer; nothing here has run on a device yet.)
  • Ctrl+Alt+P locks or frees the pointer by hand, Ctrl+Alt+M opens Porthole's in-game menu. Keys reach the game only while it is in front: not while a sheet, alert or text field of Porthole has the keyboard, and everything held is released when focus leaves.
  • Settings > Engine options > Keyboard and mouse: turn it off, mouse speed, always use the mouse to look, the pointer lock, focus, hiding the on-screen controls while a keyboard or mouse is used, and extra logging.
  • Outside a game and while the in-game menu is open the keyboard still moves around Porthole's own screens.

Changed

  • The on-screen controls step aside while a keyboard or mouse is being used (as they do for a controller); a touch brings them back until the next key or mouse movement.
1.2.122 (build 673): Red Dead Redemption 2: Social Club page sharing back on.

Fixed

  • Red Dead Redemption 2: the 1.2.121 experiment (Social Club's IPC split off) stopped the Rockstar launcher's own page from loading, so it is back on, as in 1.2.120.
1.2.121 (build 672): Red Dead Redemption 2: experiment with Social Club's page sharing.

Changed

  • Red Dead Redemption 2 starts with Social Club's IPC split switched off (MADEIRA_SC_IPC_SPLIT=0), to find out whether the game's in-game Social Club page can finish loading without it. Experiment for this game only.
1.2.120 (build 671): Red Dead Redemption 2: log capture fixed.

Fixed

  • Red Dead Redemption 2: 1.2.119 asked for a longer game-log mirror than the engine accepts (4000 lines is the most; more falls back to 400), so the log stopped before the game's Social Club phase. Back to 4000, with Chromium's own log from Social Club's helper kept on.
1.2.119 (build 670): Red Dead Redemption 2: more logging while we chase the Social Club overlay delay.

Changed

  • Red Dead Redemption 2 now writes Chromium's own log from Social Club's web helper and keeps up to 30000 log lines. In the game, Social Club's page can take over two minutes to appear and Rockstar gives up after 130 seconds; the extra log should show why. Nothing else changes.
1.2.118 (build 669): Rockstar launcher works with the new Social Club.

Fixed

  • Rockstar Games Launcher (Red Dead Redemption 2, GTA V): Social Club updated to 2.4.0.296 on 3 November and the launcher then stopped at 'timed out loading both its online and offline content', because the engine only knew the previous Social Club build. The engine now recognises both. Needs a new engine, so compatibility badges start again for it.
1.2.117 (build 668): Keep a game running in the background (experimental).

Added

  • Settings > Play > Performance > Keep a game running in the background (off by default; restart Porthole after changing it): iOS pauses an app a few seconds after you switch apps or lock the screen, which stops a game mid-load. With this on, a running game keeps going for 2, 5, 10 or 30 minutes with its sound muted, its CPU held to a share of one core (iOS ends an app that uses more for long) and its drawing held until you are back; then it pauses and a notification says so. Settings > Advanced > Engine options has the sound and CPU cap choices. Ported from Madeira.
1.2.116 (build 667): Keep compiled graphics pipelines between runs.

Added

  • Settings > Advanced (Performance > Graphics, and Engine options > Direct3D 12): Keep compiled pipelines between runs, a size limit for the archive, and Build pipelines in the background. Games compile their graphics pipelines while they run, which stutters the first time each is used; with the archive on, the next start stutters less. Off by default, applies the next time a game starts.
1.2.115 (build 666): Audio settings, a redone Steam and Epic game page, Big Picture hidden.

Added

  • Settings > Play > Sound: Game volume (50% to 200% of the level a game sends) and Bring sound back after a call (after a call, Siri, an alarm or a suspension, a game's sound stays off until the game restarts; on, Porthole activates the audio again). Both are also in Settings > Advanced > Engine options. They apply the next time a game starts.

Changed

  • A game's page in the Steam and Epic stores is redone like a library game's page: the picture as a card with the game's name on it, who made it and when, how it runs, the install, pause, update or play buttons with a smooth progress bar, and three number tiles. Held sideways or on an iPad it has two panes, the game on the left and what the store says about it on the right. The picture also sits blurred behind the page.
  • Steam Big Picture is hidden: its row in Settings > Stores > Steam shows only after seven taps on the version (Settings > Help and about) and the switch there. Its web helper has been seen to crash on the first click.
1.2.114 (build 665): Steam Big Picture no longer widens the memory window for another game.

Fixed

  • Starting Steam Big Picture (or any program run from the top of drive C, or a batch file) measured every game on the drive for the executable-window widening and widened the window to 480 MB for Final Fantasy Resonance's program, which has nothing to do with it. That changed the memory layout Madeira tested for Big Picture. A batch file and a program at the top of drive C are no longer measured.
1.2.113 (build 664): GOG Galaxy services for GOG games.

Added

  • GOG Galaxy services: a GOG game built with GOG's Galaxy SDK talks to GOG's client for its sign-in, achievements, stats, leaderboards and play time. While one of your GOG games runs, Porthole answers for that client on this device only (127.0.0.1:9977), with a token GOG mints for that one game from your GOG sign-in. It needs a game installed from GOG's own builds (one installed before this is read once, at its next start). Settings > Stores > GOG turns it off. Without a sign-in the game runs offline, as before. Ported from Madeira (Playport's Galaxy service).
1.2.112 (build 663): Steam Big Picture (experimental).

Added

  • Settings > Stores > Steam > Steam Big Picture: starts Valve's own Steam client in Big Picture mode (its gamepad interface) inside the Wine desktop. Experimental: Madeira leaves it out of its releases because it is heavy and unfinished. It needs the Steam client, its first start downloads Valve's client from Valve into a folder of its own, and it asks for a lot of memory (a 4 GB swap file and 4 GB of donor RAM, Madeira's tested lines for it). Not started by itself, and your games are not changed.
1.2.111 (build 662): The 32-bit Visual C++ runtime.

Added

  • Microsoft's 32-bit Visual C++ runtime for 32-bit programs (older games, many launchers): about 14 MB from Microsoft's own installer, checked against a pinned fingerprint and unpacked on the device. Offered in the setup guide and in Settings > Advanced > Windows components. Without it, 32-bit programs keep running on Wine's own C++ runtime. Ported from Madeira.
1.2.110 (build 661): Wine Gecko: the web engine for launchers and installers.

Added

  • Wine Gecko, the web engine Wine uses for programs that show a web page (many launchers and installers). About 110 MB from WineHQ, checked against fixed fingerprints, offered in the setup guide next to the Visual C++ runtime and .NET, and in Settings > Advanced > Windows components. Without it Wine stops on its own download window, which can hang. Ported from Madeira.
1.2.109 (build 660): Steam and Epic game pages polished.

Added

  • Three number tiles under the Install button: on Steam what it downloads, what it takes on the device and when it came out; on Epic where the game stands on the device, who made it and its build.
  • The Steam page shows the Steam store chip under the title, like Epic's.

Changed

  • The banner picture at the top of a game's store page is taller and fades into the page instead of ending in an edge.
  • The progress of a download is the same smooth bar as on the tiles, the screenshots have rounder corners and a soft shadow and give a little when pressed, and the Steam rows have an Explore heading.
1.2.108 (build 659): Moving between sections is smoother.

Added

  • A gradient line sits under the chosen section in the top bar and slides to the next one when you change section.

Changed

  • A section's content fades in when it opens (Home, Stores, Reports, Settings) instead of cutting in. It is skipped when Reduce Motion is on.
1.2.107 (build 658): Settings pages open with a proper header.

Changed

  • Every Settings page (and the pages inside Advanced and Stores) opens with its icon on a gradient tile, its name and one line that says what the page is for.
1.2.106 (build 657): Store game tiles and sheets that match the rest of the app.

Changed

  • Every store's game tile (Steam, Epic Games, GOG, itch.io, Amazon Games, EA) looks the same now and like the tiles on Home: a status as a small round icon (a ring with the percent while it downloads, a check when installed, an arrow for an update), a soft shade at the foot of the picture, a thin light edge and a shadow, and a smooth bar under the name while it downloads. The tile gives a little when pressed.
  • The sheet GOG, itch.io, Amazon Games and the EA app open for a game is redesigned: the Porthole background instead of the plain system one, the store at the top, the game's picture as a card with its name on it, a gradient Install button, red for what deletes, and a Done button.
