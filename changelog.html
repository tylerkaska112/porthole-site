What's new

What's new in Porthole

Porthole is still in early development and has not had its first public release. This is what has changed in the builds so far, in plain words, newest first.

Stores and downloads

  • One Stores tab. Steam, Epic Games, GOG, itch.io and an All games page share it, with a switcher at the top. Every game carries the logo of the store it came from, on its card and on the home screen.
  • Epic Games. Sign in on Epic's own page, then install the games your account owns that have a Windows version and play them without Epic's launcher. Epic's overlay and anti-cheat are not supported.
  • GOG. Sign in on GOG's own page and install the games you own, straight from GOG's servers where it has a build there, otherwise from GOG's setup file. Games that need GOG Galaxy's online features are not supported.
  • itch.io. Paste a personal API key made on itch.io and install the games you bought, claimed or collected, including free ones.
  • The same layout for every store: a grid of your games with a badge for what each is doing and a page for every game, with its compatibility badge, description and screenshots where the store has them.
  • One Downloads page for every store, opened by a round button that stays at the bottom right of the Stores tab. It shows what each store is downloading, the games waiting and the paused ones, with Pause, Resume, Download now, Pause all and Resume all.
  • Downloads keep going when you leave the app, and a card on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island shows the percentage, size, speed and time left with Pause, Resume and Cancel buttons. The card is an optional part of the install; without it iOS's own progress card is used.
  • Steam games on an external drive. Choose a folder on an SSD and send new Steam downloads there. This is new and has had little testing.
  • Much less lag in the Stores tab and while something downloads, and swiping in from the left edge goes back, as in any iOS app.

Security and privacy

  • Store sign-ins stay in the iOS Keychain, for this device only. Store traffic is kept out of the network cache, and sign-in pages keep no cookies.
  • Pairing files, certificates and profiles you pick are deleted from temporary storage as soon as they are read.
  • Updates are checked against the maintainer's signature before they are installed.
  • Problem reports also hide IPv6 addresses and any password, token or key that appears next to its name, and the pairing log no longer names your iPhone.

Updating the app

  • A popup while it updates itself with the steps (download, sign, install), one progress bar, and a plain warning to keep the app open until it is done.
  • Signing is several times faster, and so is sending the signed app to iOS's installer.

The engine

  • Ten open fixes from the Madeira project were taken in, among them the reduced-precision x87 switch working as its name says, a large-memory option for games that reserve a lot at start, more of Windows' own DirectX helper libraries, and fixes for launchers that start a second copy of themselves.

Look and feel

  • A new home screen. Your games sit on one shelf. With a controller connected the chosen game grows and the rest step back; on touch every game is the same size.
  • A slim top bar on iPhone and iPad, with the Porthole mark and the name next to it.
  • A game's page is short again: Play, three settings, and four doors (controls, how it runs, more options, about and files).
  • An app icon: a silver porthole looking at a sunset sea, and a night sky with a crescent moon in dark mode.
  • Dark only, in a slate blue in the Steam spirit with Porthole's own cyan.

Controllers

  • FULL and PARTIAL badges from Steam's own list, on the shelf, the Steam screen and every game's page.
  • Press the badge to see what it means and how to cover the parts a controller can't reach.
  • A Controller tab on the Steam screen that lists only the games with controller support.

Steam

  • Add an installed game back to your library at any time; it still starts through Steam.
  • A clearer Steam screen and Downloads page, with labelled buttons and one summary line.

Compatibility reports

  • Shorter lists, with the details one switch away.
  • Reports are counted per device model and per engine version, so an old result isn't passed off as a current one.

Setup and updates

  • Setup is three steps: full speed, Windows components, your games from any of the four stores, then a short Good to know page.
  • A warning in the game when memory or the swap file is nearly full, saying what to change.
  • Settings has six pages instead of fifteen.
  • Updates: versions are listed five at a time; with no certificate added you can download any version as a file.
  • Your VPN: Porthole asks before pausing it, and can turn it back on with a shortcut you make.
  • Notices on the home screen can be hidden with a small x.

Every build, newest first

The last 25 builds, exactly as the app lists them in Settings › Updates. This list is made from the project's change log.

0.50.289 (build 385): The round Downloads button is solid, so it shows on light backgrounds.

Changed

  • The Downloads button no longer shows the page through it: it has a solid background and a stronger edge.
0.50.288 (build 384): The Downloads button is now a round button at the bottom right of the Stores tab.

Added

  • A round Downloads button that stays at the bottom right of the Stores tab, over every store's library and the games' pages, and does not move when you scroll or switch stores. A ring shows the progress of what is downloading and a number shows how many downloads there are.

Changed

  • The Downloads buttons inside each store's row of controls are gone; the round button replaces them.
0.50.287 (build 383): One Downloads page for every store.

Added

  • A single Downloads page that shows what Steam, Epic Games, GOG and itch.io are downloading, the games waiting, the paused or failed ones, and GOG or itch.io setups ready to install. Open it from the Downloads button in any store (it shows all stores' progress), from the Stores list, from All games, or from a Home download notice.
  • GOG and itch.io can now be paused, resumed, moved to the front (Download now) and removed from the Downloads page, like Steam and Epic.

Changed

  • Steam and Epic no longer have a Downloads page of their own; Back returns to the store you came from.
0.50.286 (build 382): Setup now covers all four stores, says plainly what is sent, and has a Good to know page; Porthole warns you in a game when memory is nearly full.

Added

  • A Good to know page at the end of setup: the first start is slower, big games may need Swap, controllers, and Performance mode up to 120 FPS.
  • A warning over the game when the swap file is nearly full or the device is nearly out of memory, saying what to change (at most four times a session).

Changed

  • Setup's Games step and Add a game now offer GOG and itch.io as well as Steam, Epic Games and Files, and signing in to either counts as games added.
  • The privacy note in setup says exactly what Porthole sends and when.
  • Home shows GOG and itch.io downloads as well as Steam and Epic ones.
0.50.285 (build 381): Security: store sign-ins leave no copy in the network cache, picked certificates and pairing files are deleted after import, and updates are checked against the maintainer's signature.

Changed

  • Epic, GOG and itch.io (and Steam's cloud saves) now use a connection that keeps nothing on disk: no cache, no cookies. Before, iOS's shared cache could hold their tokens and API key, and a Windows program in the app could read them. The old cache is emptied once.
  • A pairing file, certificate or profile you pick is deleted from the app's temporary folder as soon as it has been read (it is kept in the Keychain); leftovers from earlier versions are removed at start.
  • Updates are installed only when they carry the maintainer's signature (builds that carry the release key; the download is also checked against the signed checksum).
  • Steam cloud saves are always downloaded over https.
  • Problem reports also hide public IPv6 addresses and any password, token or API key that appears next to its name. The pairing log no longer names your iPhone.
  • Pairing from this iPhone's Settings stops after five wrong codes.
0.50.284 (build 380): Security hardening: logins and keys are kept where Windows programs cannot read them.

Changed

  • The pairing file (the key that lets a computer debug this iPhone) is now kept in the Keychain instead of a file in the app's storage, and is moved there on first start.
  • Update certificates, their passwords and this install's report key are kept in the Keychain only; a fallback file that an earlier version could leave in the app's storage is moved into the Keychain and deleted.
  • After each Steam session, and when the app starts, the sign-in tokens and account names that Valve's own client saves inside Porthole are removed, and those folders are kept out of device backups. Porthole signs Steam in again for every game anyway.
  • Problem reports also hide GOG and itch.io account names, Steam 64-bit ids, and Epic account ids and names that appear on a game's command line.
  • Epic's sign-in page no longer keeps cookies, and Sign out clears any an earlier version kept.
  • The update server address must be https (plain http is accepted only for this device itself).
0.50.283 (build 379): Free games from your itch.io collections are listed too.

Changed

  • Stores > itch.io: games in your itch.io collections that are free (including pay-what-you-want at zero) and have a Windows download are now listed next to the games you bought or claimed, and install the same way. Refresh the list to see them.
0.50.282 (build 378): itch.io's own icon in the Stores tab.

Changed

  • Stores: the itch.io store now shows itch.io's real storefront icon instead of a drawn tile.
0.50.281 (build 377): itch.io joins the Stores tab.

Added

  • Stores: itch.io is a fourth store. Sign in with a personal API key (made on itch.io's API keys page, which opens inside Porthole), and the games you bought, claimed or got in bundles that have a Windows download are listed with their covers. Open a game, pick which download to use if there are several, and tap Install: Porthole downloads the file (pause and resume, download card and Live Activity like the other stores), unpacks the zip, finds the game's program and adds it to your library. Single-exe games work too, and installers (an .exe named setup or an .msi) run on the Wine desktop and are picked up afterwards. Browser-only games are not listed, and 7z or rar downloads are not supported yet.
0.50.280 (build 376): GOG pictures are no longer washed out.

Fixed

  • Stores > GOG: the pictures looked pale and faded to white at the bottom, because they were GOG's store-page background. They are now GOG's own card picture of each game (the one with the title), which is clean and full colour.
0.50.279 (build 375): GOG games whose program name differs in capitals now finish installing.

Fixed

  • GOG: RollerCoaster Tycoon Deluxe and other games stopped at The installer finished but RCT.exe is not there, because GOG's info file spells the program RCT.exe and the download holds RCT.EXE, and this file system is case-sensitive. The program is now found whatever its capitals. Press Try again on such a game; the files are already there.
0.50.278 (build 374): GOG games now download straight from GOG, no setup program needed.

Added

  • Stores > GOG: a game that GOG has a Galaxy build of (Fallout 2 and most of the catalogue) is downloaded straight from GOG's servers: Porthole reads the build's file list, fetches the pieces in parallel, checks every piece and file against GOG's checksums and writes the game into C:\\GOG. There is no setup program to run, so it is faster and does not depend on Wine running GOG's installer. Games without a Galaxy build still use GOG's setup file.

Fixed

  • GOG: the setup-file route stopped with a path not found popup for Fallout 2 on the Wine desktop (the setup read a damaged file path while loading shell32). Fallout 2 no longer uses it.
0.50.277 (build 373): GOG installs no longer fail with a wrong-size error.

Fixed

  • GOG: every game stopped at A downloaded file was the wrong size even though the whole file had arrived, because GOG lists file sizes rounded to whole megabytes. The download is now checked against the size the server states and GOG's own checksum.
0.50.276 (build 372): Install GOG games: Porthole downloads GOG's setup file and runs it for you.

Added

  • Stores > GOG: open a game and tap Install. Porthole downloads GOG's own setup file (with pause, resume and a checksum check, and the download card and Live Activity like Steam and Epic), runs it silently inside Wine into C:\\GOG, then adds the game to your library with the right program, and deletes the setup files. This is the route for older GOG games that have no download of their own, such as Fallout 2. Games installed this way can be uninstalled from the game's sheet or from Settings > Storage.
0.50.275 (build 371): GOG's own icon in the Stores tab.

Changed

  • Stores: the GOG store now shows GOG's real icon instead of a drawn tile, and the search box says 1 game rather than 1 games.
0.50.274 (build 370): GOG joins the Stores tab: sign in and see your library.

Added

  • Stores: GOG is a third store next to Steam and Epic Games. Sign in on GOG's own page and the games you own that have a Windows version are listed, with their pictures, search and a badge in All games. Downloading and installing from GOG is the next step, so for now each game only opens a page with a link to it on GOG.
0.50.273 (build 369): See everything that changed since the version you have.

Added

  • Settings > Updates: tap Update available (under Check for updates) to see the New, Changed and Fixed lists of every version since the one you have, each line tagged with its version, plus a one-line summary of each version and the update button at the bottom.
0.50.272 (build 368): Calmer with no connection.

Added

  • Steam, Epic Games and All games show an Offline chip when the device has no network, and keep showing the saved list.

Changed

  • With no network, refreshing the Steam or Epic library, the compatibility list and the answers to your problem reports waits quietly instead of failing with a red error; they refresh when the connection is back.
  • Settings > Updates says Offline instead of a failed check, and the Reports lists say the list needs a connection.
  • A Steam library refresh that works clears an old error.
0.50.271 (build 367): Home tells you when a Steam game you have installed has an update.

Added

  • Home shows a notice when a Steam game on the device has a newer build on Steam (Factorio has an update, or 3 Steam games have updates, with their names). Tapping it opens the Steam library, where the games with an update carry the Update badge. It comes back when another game, or a newer build of the same one, appears.

Changed

  • Porthole looks for new Steam builds each time you come back to it when its list is over six hours old, not only when it starts.
0.50.270 (build 366): Home tells you when a report was answered, and My reports is at the top.

Added

  • When the maintainer reviews or fixes a report you sent, or adds a note, Home shows a notice (Your report was fixed in 0.50.xxx, or was reviewed, with the note). Tapping it opens Report a problem. Porthole looks for answers when it opens and when you come back to it.

Changed

  • Report a problem: My reports is the first thing on the page, above the form to send a new one.
0.50.269 (build 365): A crash report now carries the right run first.

Fixed

  • After a game ended unexpectedly, the part of a report labelled as the run that ended could be the run before it (the log was read before this launch had set the finished run's log aside). It is now the run that ended, and the game's own log is added only when it is a different file.
0.50.268 (build 364): A problem report carries the whole log of the run, not a slice of it.

Changed

  • Report a problem now includes the whole log of the run that is reported (and of the run before it, or, after a crash, of the run that ended and the game's own log), cleaned of personal parts. A report sent to the server is packed first (a log shrinks about ten to one), so a 25 MB log travels as a few megabytes. A log over 60 MB keeps its first 2 MB and its last part and says what it left out. The preview row shows the start and the end and says how much is between.
0.50.267 (build 363): A report after a crash carries the crashed run, not the launch that followed it.

Added

  • When Porthole opens and finds that the last run ended while a game was running (a crash, iOS closing the app, or a force-quit), it keeps that run's log and the game's own log aside, three at a time. Settings > Report a problem then offers to include the crash (on by default), and a report uses the crashed run's log first, then the game's own log if it is a different file, then a little of the launch after it, so opening the app again, even twice, no longer loses the crash. The Report a problem page shows a ! until such a crash has been reported.

Changed

  • A sent report's upload makes room for the crashed run (about 1 MB of its end).
0.50.266 (build 362): Tap a report to read the maintainer's whole answer.

Changed

  • Settings > Report a problem > My reports: tapping a report opens it, with its status, the whole note from the maintainer, what you sent and when it was answered. Withdrawing a report is a button inside it, so a tap on the list no longer asks to withdraw.
0.50.265 (build 361): Send a problem to Porthole's server and follow what happens to it.

Added

  • Settings > Report a problem > Send it to Porthole's server: the report (what you wrote, the facts about the install and, if you leave the switch on, the cleaned end of the log) goes to the maintainer, privately. Under My reports you see each one as Open, Reviewed or Fixed in a version, with a note from the maintainer, and a number on the Settings page when something was answered. You can withdraw a report. Nothing is sent without that button and a confirmation, and what would be sent is what the preview row shows.

When the first public release is out, every version will be listed here and on the project's Releases page.
