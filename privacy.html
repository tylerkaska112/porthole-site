Privacy

Privacy

Porthole has no ads, no analytics and no account of its own. This page says exactly what leaves your device, and when. Last updated 17 October 2026.

What stays on your device

  • Your games, saves, settings and the performance log (telemetry.csv in the Files app).
  • Your pairing file, which lets Porthole switch JIT on. It is kept in the iOS Keychain, not in the Files app.
  • Your store sign-ins, if you sign in to Steam, Epic Games, GOG, Amazon Games or itch.io: a sign-in token for each (for itch.io, the API key you made there). They are kept in the iOS Keychain, for this device only, until you sign out. Passwords are never kept.
  • If you use the optional in-app updating: your signing certificate, its password and your provisioning profiles, kept in the Keychain and used only on your device.

None of this is uploaded.

Compatibility reports

A report is only sent when you tap Send. It contains:

WhatDetails
The gameFor a Steam game, its Steam app number and title. For any other game, a fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) of its main program file, plus the title you gave it. The game itself is never sent.
Your deviceModel (for example iPhone 16 Pro), iOS version, Porthole version, and the version of the game engine it runs on (a Madeira commit number).
Your answersHow it ran, whether it crashed, the tags you ticked, and which store the files came from (a fixed list, not typed).
MeasurementsAverage and low frame rate, how long you played, and how hot the device got, if you leave "Include what the app measured" on.
SettingsHow the game was started and the performance settings it used, if you leave "Share my settings" on. Launch options you typed are never shared.
Your noteOptional. A person reads it before it can appear, and notes that look like links or download sites are removed.

Each report is tied to a random key made on your device. It is not tied to your name, your Apple ID, your Steam account or any device identifier. The server does not store IP addresses; it holds them in memory for a short time to limit abuse. Reports whose files came from "somewhere else" are kept private and are never shown or counted.

You can delete your own reports in Settings ▸ Compatibility reports, and forget your reporting key there too. Reports are public: the device model, iOS version and answers are shown next to the game.

Problem reports

Settings ▸ Report a problem can make a file that describes a problem, to share wherever you like (Files, AirDrop, Discord, GitHub). Making the file sends nothing. Only if you also tap Send it to Porthole's server and confirm, the same report goes to the project's server. It contains:

WhatDetails
Your wordsWhat you typed in the box, if anything.
About your installPorthole version and build, the engine version, your device model, iOS version, memory and free space, how Porthole was installed (which parts are in it), how JIT is switched on, and how many games are in your library.
The logThe whole log of the run you are reporting (after a crash: the run that ended, and the game's own log) and of the run before it, unless you switch it off. A log is a few megabytes, up to a few tens; it is packed before it is sent. The app removes folder names inside its own storage, your Steam, Epic, GOG and itch.io account names, e-mail addresses, public IP addresses and any password, token or key that appears next to its name from it before anything is saved or sent. A log can still mention the games you run and technical details of the engine. You can open "Look at exactly what would be shared" and read the whole report first.

A problem report is private: it is not shown to anyone else and not counted anywhere. It can be read by the project's maintainer, and by your own install, which shows you its status (open, reviewed, or fixed in a version) and a short note from the maintainer if there is one. It is tied to the same random key as compatibility reports, not to your name, your Apple ID, your store accounts or any device identifier. The server does not store IP addresses; it holds them in memory for a short time to limit abuse.

The whole log is needed because the cause of a crash is often far from its last lines. The log part of a report is deleted 30 days after the report is marked fixed, and a fixed report is deleted after a year. You can withdraw a report you sent at any time in Settings ▸ Report a problem ▸ My reports; it is then deleted from the server.

Showing badges

To show compatibility badges, Porthole downloads the public list of games that have reports for your engine version and your device model, and matches your own games against it on your device. The server is not told which games you have: it sees only a request for the list, with your device model and engine version. You can switch badges off in Settings ▸ Compatibility reports.

Game stores

Porthole talks to a store only to list the games you own and to download them, and only after you sign in. Sign-in tokens go to that store's own servers and nowhere else, and they are kept in the iOS Keychain. Porthole does not keep a cache or cookies of that traffic.

  • Steam: you can sign in with a QR code that you approve in the Steam mobile app, so Porthole never sees your password, or with your account name and password: the password is encrypted before it leaves the device, sent once to Steam and never saved. The app keeps only a sign-in token. With it, Porthole asks Steam, on your behalf and only when you use a feature, for your game library, your Steam Cloud save files for a game, the versions and add-ons of a game, your achievements for a game, and the Workshop items you browse, subscribe to or download. All of it comes from and goes to Valve's servers and is kept only on your device; none of it is sent to Porthole's servers. Achievement pop-ups come from files Steam's own client writes on your device. Games and cover art come from Valve's servers. To show which games work with a controller, Porthole asks Valve's store about the Steam app numbers of the games on your screen; the answers are kept on your device for a week. If Valve's own client runs inside Porthole it can save its own sign-in; Porthole removes it when the session ends.
  • Epic Games and GOG: you sign in on the store's own web page, so Porthole never sees your password. The page keeps no cookies after you close it.
  • Amazon Games: you sign in on Amazon's own page, so Porthole never sees your password. The app keeps only a sign-in token and uses it to list the games you own and to download them from Amazon's servers. The page keeps no cookies after you close it.
  • itch.io: you make a personal API key on itch.io and paste it into Porthole. It is sent only to itch.io, as the credential of requests to list and download your games.

Porthole is unofficial. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Valve, Epic Games, GOG, Amazon or itch.io.

Updates

Porthole can check for new builds on its maintainer's update server, and download them. That server sees your IP address, as any website does when you visit it, and nothing else about you. When you update from inside the app, Porthole first checks the maintainer's signature on the new build, then signs it on your device with your own certificate and installs it on your device. Your certificate, profiles and the signed copy are never uploaded anywhere.

Pairing and the tunnel

Pairing and the JIT tunnel use connections that stay on your device. The VPN entry Porthole creates is a local loopback: no traffic from other apps goes through it. iOS runs one VPN at a time, so before it turns its own tunnel on Porthole checks whether another VPN is on (it cannot tell which one), asks you, and afterwards can run a shortcut you choose to turn your VPN back on. Nothing about this is sent anywhere.

Where reports are stored

Compatibility reports and problem reports are stored on a computer run by the project's maintainer, not with an advertising or analytics company, and are not sold or shared.

Your choices

  • Turn off badges and the "How did it run?" prompt in Settings ▸ Compatibility reports.
  • Delete reports you sent, or forget your reporting key, in the same place.
  • Share a problem report as a file instead of sending it to the server, or switch the log off before you send, in Settings ▸ Report a problem. Withdraw one you sent under My reports.
  • Delete the app to remove everything stored on your device.

Contact

Porthole is a one-person project and does not publish an email address. For questions and help, use the Discord or the project's issue forms. For a security problem, or a request about content, use GitHub's private Report a vulnerability button under the repository's Security tab.

Porthole is not aimed at children and does not knowingly collect information from them. If this page changes in a way that matters, the date at the top will change.
